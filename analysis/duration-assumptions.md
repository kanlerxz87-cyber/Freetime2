# Game-duration inputs for time-window estimates

These are average elapsed event durations, not guaranteed game end times. They support indicative time windows only; overtime, weather delays, postseason format, and pre-/post-game radio programming can extend the on-air period.

| Sport | Estimate used | Evidence | Status / limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| MLB regular season | 158 minutes (2 h 38 m) | [MLB's 2025 season report](https://www.mlb.com/news/mlb-average-game-time-under-three-hours-third-straight-year) reports a 2:38 average for nine-inning games. | Official MLB historical mean; not specific to future games or extra-inning / postseason games. |
| NFL | 184 minutes (3 h 04 m) | [NFL official rules](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf) state 60 minutes of regulation clock. [NFL Analytics](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-how-long-is-an-nfl-game.html) reports 2025 elapsed-time analysis around 3:04. | Elapsed-time mean is secondary analysis of play-by-play, not an NFL-published statistic. Estimate is 184 minutes; OT may be longer. |
| FBS/NCAAF | 207 minutes (3 h 27 m) | [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/despite-running-fewer-plays-college-football-games-are-actually-getting-longer-so-whos-to-blame/) reports the 2024 mean as 3:27 and references NCAA game-length tracking. | Secondary report; broad FBS reference, not specific to Stanford, Cal, future seasons, bowls or overtime. |

The rendered day view reports approximate end/free intervals only for scheduled events with a confirmed start time. For any schedule row with an unknown start, it does not invent an interval. Seasonal windows are conservative model ranges, not specific event calendars.
